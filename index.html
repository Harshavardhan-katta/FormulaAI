The problem

Good formulas still fail in unpredictable ways.

Controlled-release nanopesticides can cut chemical use and slow resistance — but the same carrier particle releases its active ingredient at different speeds depending on size, coating, soil, and weather. There's still no fast, reliable way to know how a formulation will perform before it's built and tested.

  • No standard framework exists to predict release behaviour ahead of lab trials.
  • Pest resistance to conventional chemicals is now the norm, not the exception.
  • Adoption is blocked by cost, complexity, and a knowledge gap — not just chemistry.
How it works

One system, two audiences.

Part 1

For researchers

Enter a formulation's parameters — nanoparticle type, particle size, coating, soil pH, moisture, temperature — and the model predicts how it will actually behave.

  • Release rate over time
  • Effectiveness against the target pest
  • Environmental risk score
Part 2

For farmers

No chemistry knowledge required. Enter what you already know — crop, pest, soil type, pH, moisture — and get a straightforward recommendation.

  • Matched against the formulation database
  • Ranked by effectiveness, then safety
  • Clear dosage and application guidance
What it predicts

Three outputs, one recommendation.

01

Release rate

Percentage of active ingredient released per day, modelled across a 28-day window.

regression · time-series
02

Effectiveness

Low, medium, or high performance against the specified target pest.

classification
03

Environmental risk

A leaching and toxicity estimate, so lower-risk formulations surface first.

ordinal scoring
04

Farmer recommendation

The three predictions above, translated into one plain-language suggestion for the field.

rule-based matching
